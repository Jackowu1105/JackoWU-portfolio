## The Challenge

### User Pain Points

**Attendees:** Carrying multiple payment methods was inconvenient. No way to track spending or check balance mid-event.

**Vendors:** Most were small business owners aged 45+ with minimal smartphone experience. Direct sunlight made low-contrast screens unreadable. Ambient noise (85+ dB) drowned out audio feedback. Vendors wore gloves, making small tap targets unusable.

**Environment:** Wifi reliability at outdoor venue averaged only 60% uptime during peak hours.

## Research & Discovery

I conducted **observational studies** at the previous year's festival (shadowing 8 vendors for 2 hours each), **vendor interviews** (n=12 returning stall operators), **attendee survey** (n=200 past festival-goers), and **competitive analysis** of RFID systems at Coachella, Glastonbury, and local events.

### Design Principles

1. **Glanceable** — All critical info visible in 1 second
2. **Tactile** — Large tap targets (min 60x60pt) for gloved hands
3. **Resilient** — Offline-first architecture with sync queue
4. **Forgiving** — Undo/refund flow for cashier errors

## Design Process

### Information Architecture

I mapped 3 primary user flows: **Token Sale** (80% of use cases) — RFID Tap → Amount Selection → Confirmation → Receipt; **Balance Check** — RFID Tap → Display Remaining Balance; **Transaction History** — View Daily Summary.

### Wireframing & Iteration

**Version 1** — Standard form layout overwhelmed non-tech vendors. 4/6 made input errors during simulation.

**Version 2** — Quick-select buttons only. Faster for common amounts but inflexible for custom pricing.

**Version 3 (Final)** — Hybrid approach combining quick-select buttons for common amounts with a custom keypad. 40% faster transaction time vs. V2.

### Key Design Decisions

**Single-page transaction interface:** Multi-step wizards required too much navigation under pressure. 85% of vendors completed training in under 10 minutes.

**Multi-modal feedback (sound + haptic + visual):** Noisy environment made visual-only feedback insufficient. Zero failed transactions due to premature card removal.

**Offline queue with visual indicator:** Research showed 40% wifi failure during peak hours. Transactions stored locally, synced when connection restored. System maintained 99.7% uptime.

### Usability Testing

Conducted 3 rounds: **Lab testing** (n=6) identified confusion with refund flow; **Field testing** at rehearsal event (n=20 vendors, 100 real transactions) discovered sunlight readability issues; **Final walkthrough** fine-tuned button sizing and contrast.

## Visual Design

**Color Palette:** Tourism Board Red (#D71920) for brand consistency, high-visibility Green (#00C853) for success states, tested for WCAG AAA compliance. Minimum 18pt for body text optimized for outdoor readability.

**Components:** Designed 12 reusable components including transaction buttons with 60x60pt minimum touch targets, RFID scan indicators with pulsing animation, and numeric displays with 48pt bold typography.

**Accessibility:** Passed iOS Accessibility Inspector audit, tested with ColorOracle for color blindness simulations, VoiceOver support for visually impaired staff.

## Impact

The system processed **62,000+ transactions** across 4 days with **22-second average transaction time** (63% faster than cash), **99.7% system uptime**, and **8-minute average vendor training time**. Post-event survey showed **94% vendor satisfaction**. The Tourism Board extended the contract for 2017-2018 festivals.

## Reflections

This project taught me the importance of designing for extreme contexts. Many UX principles I learned in controlled environments broke down under real-world festival conditions. The key was empathizing with vendors' constraints — not everyone is a digital native, and good UX meets users where they are.
