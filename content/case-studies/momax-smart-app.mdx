## Problem Definition

### User Pain Points

Through user interviews and usability testing of the legacy app, I identified critical issues:

- **Complex device setup** — 78% of users failed to complete device pairing on first attempt
- **Slow control response** — Average 4.2 seconds to control a device
- **Poor information architecture** — Users couldn't find scene/schedule features buried 3 levels deep
- **Inconsistent cross-platform experience** — iOS and Android felt like different products

### Business Problems

- **Low retention:** Only 32% of users remained active after 30 days
- **High support costs:** 400+ monthly tickets related to app setup issues
- **Missed opportunities:** Voice control and automation features were underutilized

## Research & Insights

### Research Methods

I conducted **user interviews (n=12)** with existing MOMAX customers, **usability testing** observing 8 users attempting common tasks, **competitive analysis** of 5 leading smart home apps, and **analytics review** of 6 months of user behavior data.

### Key Insights

**Mental model mismatch** — Users think about devices by physical location ("bedroom lights"), but the old app organized by device type ("all lights"), causing cognitive friction.

**Speed is critical** — For IoT control, delays over 2 seconds felt "broken" even when technically functional.

**Setup is the make-or-break moment** — 67% of users who failed initial setup never returned to the app.

## Design Process

### Information Architecture Redesign

Restructured around user mental models with 4 primary sections:

1. **Home** — Quick access dashboard with favorite devices and one-tap controls
2. **Rooms** — Spatial organization aligning with how users think about their devices
3. **Automation** — Combined scenes and schedules with clearer labeling
4. **Settings** — Consolidated account, device management, and preferences

**Validation:** Card sorting exercise with 10 users showed 85% agreement with new IA.

### Key Design Decisions

**Navigation Pattern:** Switched from hamburger menu to bottom tab bar navigation. Usability testing showed 2.5x faster task completion vs. the old pattern, and reduced navigation depth from 3+ levels to maximum 2.

**Device Setup Flow:** Reduced from 7 steps to 3 steps by combining pairing and scanning with smart auto-detection, and moving advanced options to optional post-setup.

### Usability Testing & Iteration

**Round 1** — Wireframe testing (n=6): 100% task completion for adding a new smart plug (vs. 22% with the old app). Renamed "Scenes" to "Automation" and added swipe gestures between room views.

**Round 2** — High-fidelity prototype (n=8): 87% average task success rate (vs. 45% old app). Refined platform-specific UI patterns and added customizable quick-access widget.

## Visual Design

### Design System

Created 42 reusable components in Figma including device cards (8 variants), control widgets, status indicators, navigation elements, form inputs, and modals.

**Color Palette:** MOMAX Blue (#0066FF) for primary actions, with semantic colors for device states — green for "on", gray for "off", red for alerts, orange for "connecting".

**Platform Considerations:** Followed Apple HIG and Material Design 3 respectively, with shared IA and flows but adapted visual language. Included WCAG AA compliance, voice control integration, and Dynamic Type support.

## Reflections

Early and frequent testing saved time — lo-fi prototypes caught major navigation issues that would have required significant rework during development. The design system investment paid off, as both platforms shared 80% of components. Involving developers earlier would have identified backend constraints sooner.
