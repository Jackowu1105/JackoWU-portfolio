## Problem Definition

A hackathon is a tech event — the identity needs to feel energetic and forward-looking, not corporate. But it also has to work across an extreme range of sizes, from a wall-size LED display to a printed certificate, and stay legible and consistent at every scale.

### Design Goals

- **Tech-event energy** — a blue-purple system that reads as forward-looking, not corporate
- **Scale from wall to paper** — the same identity works on a 2048×1280 LED wall and a printed certificate
- **Legibility at distance** — entrance signage readable from across a hall

## Design Process

### Visual Direction

I explored directions around data, AI, and forward momentum, and refined toward a blue-purple tech system — energetic enough for a hackathon, credible enough for an AIFT-branded event.

### Materials

- **LED wall backdrop (2048×1280)** — the event's main visual anchor, tested at wall scale
- **Entrance TV signage (1920×1080 and 3840×2106)** — legible at distance, guiding participants to the venue
- **Certificates** — the takeaway, testing the brand at a small, keepsake scale
- **Logo lockup and divider system** — extending the identity to print and signage

## Visual Design

### Color

A blue-purple tech system — a bright sky blue (`#9BC7F5`) paired with a deep blue (`#4566D7`) and a vivid purple (`#927BDE`), anchored by a deep navy (`#0F1F82`) for type and structure. The blue-purple pairing reads as modern and tech-forward without feeling corporate.

### Typography

A bold sans-serif with a strong hierarchy — large display weights for the event title (legible at distance on the LED wall), a clean sans for body and certificate text.

## Reflections

A hackathon identity is a test of energy and scale — the same system has to feel like a tech event on a wall-size LED display and still reproduce cleanly on a printed certificate. Building the blue-purple system up front meant every material was a configuration of the identity, not a fresh design problem.
