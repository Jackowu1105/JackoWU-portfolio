## Problem Definition

A premium office building app serves four distinct user groups — tenants, visitors, building management, and merchants — each with different needs. The design had to serve all four without any one group's workflow dominating the interface.

### User Pain Points

- **Tenants:** inefficient request management with no tracking, no visibility into request status, complex visitor access requiring 24-hour advance notice, and an unused loyalty program
- **Visitors:** cumbersome check-in at the front desk causing lobby congestion, with paper-based logs raising privacy concerns
- **Building management:** requests scattered across phone logs, emails, and paper forms, with front-desk staff spending most of their time on routine requests
- **Merchants:** no direct channel to reach office tenants for the loyalty program

### Design Goals

- **Self-service for tenants** — submit and track maintenance requests without calling
- **Frictionless visitor access** — pre-register visitors so check-in takes seconds, not minutes
- **Activate the loyalty program** — make earning and redeeming points visible and effortless
- **Centralise data for management** — one system instead of scattered phone logs and emails

## Design Process

### Research & Discovery

I conducted **stakeholder interviews** with 8 building managers, front desk staff, and executives, **tenant interviews** with 15 tenants from different companies (observing 10+ maintenance request submissions and visitor check-ins in person), **competitive analysis** of 6 premium office apps (WeWork, Equinox, The Great Room), and a **tenant survey** with 120 responses.

### Key Findings

- **Multiple user groups, different needs** — employees need quick access to building services; visitors need simple check-in; admins need oversight; merchants want exposure for the loyalty program
- **Mobile-first mindset** — most tenants prefer a mobile app over a web portal; most requests are made during work hours from office desks
- **Trust & transparency matter** — users want real-time status updates and visibility into who processed their requests
- **Loyalty program had potential** — many tenants were interested in earning points from building merchants, but the main barriers were lack of awareness and a complicated redemption process

### Information Architecture

Organised four major feature sets into a 4-tab navigation: **Home** (dashboard with quick actions), **Services** (request management), **Access** (Smart Pass and Visitor Management), and **Rewards** (loyalty program and merchant directory). I used progressive disclosure to keep the main interface clean — each tab is a focused surface, not a dumping ground.

### Usability Testing

**Round 1 (low-fidelity)** — Several participants couldn't find the Visitor Management feature (it was buried too deep). I moved it to a top-level "Access" tab and added onboarding screens explaining the loyalty program.

**Round 2 (high-fidelity)** — Most participants completed all primary tasks successfully. The System Usability Scale (SUS) score landed in the top grade range, and task completion time fell sharply versus the old manual process.

## Visual Design

### Design System

A reusable component library with light/dark mode support.

**Color:** A light blue-grey surface (`#EDF0F5` / `#D6DFE8`) for a clean, premium feel, with a dark slate (`#454549`) for text and primary actions. The palette keeps the interface calm and professional, suited to a premium office-building audience.

## Outcomes & Feedback

The app unified maintenance requests, QR access, visitor management, and a loyalty program into one experience; tenant adoption was strong, request resolution sped up, front-desk call volume dropped, and the loyalty program's participation grew markedly. The project was recognised in a property-industry awards programme.

## Reflections

Designing for multiple user groups (tenants, visitors, management, merchants) in one app required finding the "win-win-win" feature — the loyalty program connected tenant engagement, merchant exposure, and management insight at once. Progressive disclosure kept the interface clean despite the breadth of features. The 4-tab IA with progressive disclosure was the structural decision that made the breadth manageable: each tab has a clear purpose, and nothing leaks across.
