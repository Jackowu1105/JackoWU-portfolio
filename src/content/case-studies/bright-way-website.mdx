## Problem Definition

A corporate website is the first impression for prospective clients. If a visitor can't understand what the company does within seconds, they leave. The challenge was to communicate clearly without overselling — the site needed to feel credible, not flashy.

### Design Goals

- **Communicate what the company does in seconds** — clear value proposition above the fold
- **Guide visitors to the right page without friction** — intuitive navigation, no dead ends
- **Responsive across all devices** — composed layout from desktop to mobile

## Design Process

### Structure & Wireframing

I started from the questions a prospective client asks — what does the company do, what has it delivered, how do I reach them — and built the site structure around those answers. Wireframes established the page hierarchy and content flow before any visual design began.

### Responsive Design

Designed for desktop first, then scaled down to mobile with breakpoints that keep the layout composed at every size. The navigation simplifies gracefully on mobile without losing any content.

## Visual Design

### Design System

**Color:** A white base (`#FFFFFF` / `#F3F1F1`) with neutral greys (`#767679` / `#A5A6A8`) for structure and typography — a restrained palette that lets content and brand photography lead.

## Reflections

Corporate website design rewards restraint. The site's job is to disappear and let the company's message land — every decorative impulse I resisted made the result more credible. Starting from the client's questions (not the company's org chart) kept the structure honest and the navigation obvious.
