## Overview

Across the estates I worked on at CardApp, the work came in two halves that fit together. First, the **app UI** — the resident-facing interface each estate's community app is built on: estate announcements, facility and service information, and the day-to-day tools that make the app worth opening. Second, the **A5 leaflet** — the printed push that puts the app in front of a resident and shows the real UI inside. Rather than repeat the UI screens separately in a portfolio, each A5 leaflet carries the estate's own screens inside the print: the flyer front opens on the product, the back walks through what it does.

## App UI Design

The estate apps all share one CardApp framework, then adapt to each property's identity — a resident who switches estates still recognises the app, while each estate feels its own. The UI leads with what a resident actually needs day to day: a clear home that surfaces estate announcements, quick access to facility and service booking, and a layout that reads comfortably on a phone in one hand.

- **One framework, many estates** — consistent navigation and interaction patterns across every estate app, so the product family stays coherent
- **Estate identity, not estate wallpaper** — each property gets its own colour and visual treatment inside the shared framework, matching the estate's positioning
- **Resident-first hierarchy** — announcements, bookings, and services surface first; the everyday use is the design priority

## Design Direction

- **The leaflet is the app's front door** — each A5 flyer shows the real UI inside, so print and product read as one
- **Estate-localised, system-consistent** — every estate gets its own voice while the leaflet family stays recognisably CardApp
- **Front and back as one arc** — the front sells the app at a glance, the back walks through the screens that make it worth keeping
