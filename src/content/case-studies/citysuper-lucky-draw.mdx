## Research & Discovery

I analysed competitor campaigns to identify common patterns and pain points — most asked for too much information upfront and suffered high form abandonment, and mobile accounted for most of the traffic. I also conducted user interviews with c!ty'super customers.

### User Needs

- "I want to know what I could win before signing up" — need for upfront prize visibility
- "I don't trust these draws are actually random" — need for transparency and trust signals
- "I don't have time to fill out long forms" — need for quick, low-effort participation

## Design Process

### Key Design Decisions

**Progressive disclosure** — Split the experience into 3 steps: browse products first (engages users before asking for info), then provide basic info (name + email only), then receive instant confirmation. Engaging users with content before the form markedly improves completion.

**Visual product gallery with smart filters** — A card-based gallery with high-quality product images, category filters, and multi-select with a visual "selected" state. Selection was limited to a few products to prevent decision paralysis.

**Trust-building elements** — A live participant counter on the landing page, clear T&Cs with the draw date, a confirmation email with a unique entry ID, and a "Your entry #XXXX has been recorded" message.

### Wireframe Iterations

- **Concept A** (single-page scroll) — felt too long
- **Concept B** (multi-step wizard) — felt too slow
- **Concept C** (hybrid: sticky product selector + wizard flow) — best feedback ✅

### Visual Design

A dark, premium backdrop (`#1C1710` / `#32291C`) to make product photography and prize visuals pop, with warm neutral accents (`#494E5C` / `#777C8F`) for secondary surfaces — matching c!ty'super's premium supermarket aesthetic. Mobile-first, designed for a small viewport with touch-friendly targets and high-contrast CTAs.

## Usability & Iteration

I tested the flow with a small group of c!ty'super customers, focusing on the entry form and product-selection step. The hybrid sticky selector (Concept C) tested best — users could browse and pick without losing their place, and the minimal-field form kept completion smooth.

## Outcomes & Feedback

The campaign drove strong participation and built a sizable email marketing database, with most entries completed on mobile. The design system was reused for several subsequent promotional campaigns.

## Reflections

Progressive disclosure reduces friction dramatically. Trust signals matter — the live participant counter and unique entry IDs significantly improved user confidence. Reducing form fields to the essentials had the biggest single impact on completion.
