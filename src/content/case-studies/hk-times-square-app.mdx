## Problem Definition

A mall app is two products wearing one name:

- **Shoppers** arrive with a goal — a store, a restaurant, an offer — and want it resolved in seconds, usually while walking
- **Staff** run operational workflows that demand clarity and speed over long shifts, from maintenance tasks to event coordination

A single generic interface would serve neither. The design split into an end-user app and a staff app, sharing a visual language but each shaped around its own tasks.

### Design Goals

- **Shopper side:** resolve a goal in seconds — find a store, see an offer, book a service, all while walking
- **Staff side:** operational depth with clarity and speed — task lists, status updates, and checklists for one-handed use during busy shifts
- **One system, two apps** — shared component library so the brand feels consistent while each interface fits its user's hands and pace

## Design Process

### End-User App

The shopper experience centres on three moments:

1. **Before the visit** — browse offers, events, and directory from home
2. **Inside the mall** — wayfinding and store discovery on the move
3. **On the spot** — services like parking, bookings, and promotions

The interface uses large imagery, a searchable directory, and prominent offer surfaces — everything reachable within a couple of taps while walking.

### Staff App

The staff side is a working tool, not a showcase: task lists, status updates, and operational checklists with clear states and quick actions — designed for one-handed use during busy shifts.

## Visual Design

### Design System

A clean, light system that lets mall content — photography, offers, brand colours — lead.

**Color:** White and light neutral surfaces (`#FFFFFF` / `#F3F1F1`) with muted greys (`#767679` / `#A5A6A8`) for structure, keeping the interface quiet so mall imagery and promotions stand out.

## Reflections

Dual-sided design is about respecting two very different kinds of attention. Shoppers give you seconds and a thumb; staff give you a whole shift and one hand. Splitting the apps — while keeping one underlying system — let each side be optimised without compromise, and the shared component library kept the brand coherent across both. The key insight: **shoppers scan, staff tap** — designing for that difference is what made each app feel native to its user.
