## Problem Definition

Hong Kong Land's residential estates each have their own identity — The Horizon, Eden Manor, and Mount Verdant — but building three completely separate apps would be expensive to maintain and inconsistent in quality. The challenge was to give each estate a distinct digital presence while keeping the underlying system shared and maintainable.

### Design Goals

- **Estate identity over generic branding** — residents don't think of themselves as "Hong Kong Land users"; they live in The Horizon, or Eden Manor, or Mount Verdant
- **Shared core, flexible skin** — identical core flows (facilities, notices, service requests) with per-estate theming
- **Premium feel** — each app should match the quality of the physical property

## Design Process

### One System, Three Identities

Rather than designing three unrelated apps, I built a shared structure — the same core screens, navigation, and resident services — and layered each estate's identity on top through colour, imagery, and typography. Each estate's app reads as its own product; the shared system keeps it consistent and efficient to maintain.

### Resident Services

The core flows are shared across all three estates: facility booking, community notices, service requests, and visitor access. Each estate's content loads dynamically based on the resident's estate, so the app always shows relevant information — The Horizon's events, Eden Manor's facilities, Mount Verdant's notices.

## Visual Design

### Design System

A light, premium system: white and soft neutral surfaces (`#FFFFFF` / `#EDF0F5`) with blue-grey accents (`#D6DFE8`) and dark slate (`#454549`) for type — an understated base that lets each estate's photography and identity colours take the lead.

## Reflections

Multi-estate design is a theming problem as much as a UX one. Getting the shared core right first — before any estate's identity entered the picture — meant the visual flexibility never threatened the usability. The lesson carried forward into later property-app work: build the system, then let the brand dress it.
