## Problem Definition

### Key Challenges

- **Time Scarcity:** Attendees had only 2-hour windows to view 200+ posters
- **Lack of Filtering:** No way to filter by research topic, institution, or author
- **Exhibitor Engagement:** Booths needed a reason to attract visitors beyond passive browsing

## Design Process

### Information Architecture

**ePoster Browsing (Kiosk):**
Home → Browse by Topic / Search / My Bookmarks / Trending Posters

**Lucky Draw (Tablet at Booths):**
Scan Badge QR → Auto-fill Info → 3-Question Survey → Submit → Confirmation + Entry #

### Key Design Decisions

**Kiosk Instead of Mobile App:** Eliminates download barrier. Attendees could walk up and start browsing immediately. Less personalized experience but significantly higher adoption rate.

**Questionnaire + Lucky Draw Combination:** Gamification increases survey completion rates. Attendees motivated by prize incentives to engage with exhibitors. Mitigated risk of insincere responses by keeping questions short (3 multiple-choice) and relevant.

**Large Touch Targets (44x44pt minimum):** Accommodates users with limited dexterity and crowded venue conditions.

### Design Iteration

Initial grid layout showing 20 posters per screen felt overwhelming. Switched to list view with large preview images and clear categorization by medical specialty.

Manual form entry for lucky draw was too slow during peak hours. Implemented QR code scanning of attendee badges with auto-populated fields and 3 quick multiple-choice questions.

## Visual Design

Aligned with CUHK Medical Faculty branding with professional color palette. Minimum 16pt body text and 24pt headings optimized for kiosk viewing distance of 1-2 feet. Universal icons (search, bookmark, back) to overcome language barriers.

**Accessibility:** WCAG AA compliance with 4.5:1 contrast ratio, large touch targets (44x44pt minimum), fonts optimized for kiosk viewing distance.

## Impact

The low-friction kiosk approach removed barriers to entry. The lucky draw gamification successfully drove survey completion and booth traffic. The client requested to expand the system to other CUHK conferences. Multiple exhibitors requested to replicate the lucky draw feature for IDDF 2017.

## Reflections

This project taught me to design for context — conference environments demand low-friction, high-visibility solutions. A mobile app would have failed due to download barriers. Balancing needs of three groups (attendees, exhibitors, organizers) required finding the "win-win-win" feature — the lucky draw was exactly that.
