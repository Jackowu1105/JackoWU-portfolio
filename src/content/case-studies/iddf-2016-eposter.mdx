## Problem Definition

### Key Challenges

- **Time scarcity** — attendees had only short windows to view many posters
- **Lack of filtering** — no way to filter by research topic, institution, or author
- **Exhibitor engagement** — booths needed a reason to attract visitors beyond passive browsing

## Design Process

### Information Architecture

**ePoster browsing (kiosk):** Home → Browse by Topic / Search / My Bookmarks / Trending Posters

**Lucky draw (tablet at booths):** Scan Badge QR → Auto-fill Info → 3-Question Survey → Submit → Confirmation + Entry #

### Key Design Decisions

**Kiosk instead of mobile app** — Eliminates the download barrier. Attendees could walk up and start browsing immediately. Less personalised, but a far higher adoption rate.

**Questionnaire + lucky draw combination** — Gamification increases survey completion. Attendees are motivated by prize incentives to engage with exhibitors. I mitigated the risk of insincere responses by keeping the questions short (3 multiple-choice) and relevant.

**Large touch targets** — Accommodates users with limited dexterity and crowded-venue conditions.

### Design Iteration

An initial grid layout showing many posters per screen felt overwhelming. I switched to a list view with large preview images and clear categorisation by medical specialty.

Manual form entry for the lucky draw was too slow during peak hours. I implemented QR-code scanning of attendee badges with auto-populated fields and 3 quick multiple-choice questions.

## Visual Design

### Color

A light, professional base (`#F0F0F1` / `#FEFFFF`) aligned with CUHK Medical Faculty branding, with warm gold accents (`#FDD676` / `#A77C30`) for the lucky-draw energy and call-to-action highlights. The palette stays academic and calm for poster browsing while letting the lucky-draw moments feel festive.

### Typography & Icons

Generous body and heading text optimised for kiosk viewing distance, and universal icons (search, bookmark, back) to overcome language barriers.

### Accessibility

WCAG AA compliance with 4.5:1 contrast ratio, large touch targets, and fonts optimised for kiosk viewing distance.

## Outcomes & Feedback

The low-friction kiosk approach removed barriers to entry, and the lucky-draw gamification successfully drove survey completion and booth traffic. The client requested to expand the system to other CUHK conferences, and several exhibitors asked to replicate the lucky-draw feature.

## Reflections

This project taught me to design for context — conference environments demand low-friction, high-visibility solutions. A mobile app would have failed due to download barriers. Balancing the needs of three groups (attendees, exhibitors, organisers) required finding the "win-win-win" feature — the lucky draw was exactly that.
