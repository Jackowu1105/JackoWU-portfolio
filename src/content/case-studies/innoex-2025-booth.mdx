## Problem Definition

An exhibition booth is a physical space where two products compete for attention. The challenge was to give FinSightsAI and TOLO each their own presence without the booth feeling fragmented — and to extend that balance to the leaflets and banners.

### Design Goals

- **Two products, one booth** — each product gets its own voice, the booth stays unified
- **Consistent across materials** — booth, A4 leaflets, and 850×2000 pull-up banners all read as one AIFT presence
- **Type, colour, and hierarchy carry across print and signage**

## Design Process

### Visual Direction

I established the AIFT visual language as the unifying layer — consistent type, colour, and hierarchy — then gave each product its own section and accent within that system. The booth layout separates the two products physically while the shared visual language keeps them unified.

### Materials

- **Booth design** — unifying FinSightsAI and TOLO under one AIFT visual language
- **A4 leaflets** — one per product, with consistent type and hierarchy
- **850×2000 pull-up banners** — paired with the leaflets, legible at distance

## Visual Design

### Color

A light, airy blue system — a soft sky blue (`#D9EEFA` / `#A4D5F1`) as the surface, with a confident mid-blue (`#5EB7E5` / `#6994CC`) for emphasis and product accents. The palette is consistent across both products while allowing each to carry its own accent within the system.

### Typography

A clean sans-serif with a strong hierarchy — large display weights for product names and booth signage, a highly legible sans for leaflet body text.

## Reflections

Exhibition design is spatial storytelling — two products sharing one booth need their own space but also a shared frame. Locking the AIFT visual language first meant the booth, leaflets, and banners all fell out of the same system, and each product got its own voice without the booth feeling split.
