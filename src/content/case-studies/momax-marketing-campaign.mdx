## Problem Definition

A marketing campaign lives across wildly different surfaces — a Facebook post on a phone screen, a collectible token in someone's hand, and a three-metre EV charger backdrop at a charging station. The challenge was to make every touchpoint read as the same campaign instantly, without designing each piece from scratch.

### Design Goals

- **One campaign, many surfaces** — every touchpoint reads as the same campaign instantly
- **Flex across sizes and mediums** — from social feed to large-format print
- **System, not one-offs** — each piece is a configuration of the campaign system, not a fresh design problem

## Design Process

### Campaign System

I established the campaign's visual language first — colour, typography, and the core motif — then applied it across the full material list:

- **Social** — Facebook posts sized and composed for the feed
- **Event print** — posters and collectible tokens
- **Digital surfaces** — app banners and in-app product graphics
- **Large format** — EV charger backdrop
- **Handout print** — A5 leaflet and A4 posters

## Visual Design

### Design System

MOMAX brand blue (`#0195E2` / `#02A4DF`) leads the campaign identity, set against clean white surfaces with deep blue (`#1A2F47`) for structure — high contrast for social feeds and readable at distance for large-format print.

## Reflections

Campaign design is system design under a deadline. Locking the visual language early — before the first Facebook post went out — meant the token, the backdrop, and the leaflet all fell out of the same system without rework. The materials list looked intimidating on day one; by the end, it was just the same idea wearing different sizes.
