## Problem Definition

A product demo video sits at the top of the sales funnel — on the product page, in app-store previews, on social. Its job is to show the device doing what it promises, fast enough to hold attention and clear enough that a viewer who skips ahead still gets the point.

### Design Goals

- **The product is the star** — motion graphics explain, they never compete with the device on screen
- **Works on mute** — social feeds autoplay without sound, so captions and graphics carry the message
- **First three seconds decide** — the hook appears immediately; details follow in the body of the cut

## Design Process

### The Edit

My role was to take raw product footage and shape it into a demo that sells:

- **Editing** — cutting footage to a tight, purposeful rhythm: establish the product, show it in action, land the key feature, end with the brand
- **Motion graphics** — labels, callouts, and animated captions that explain what the device is doing at each moment, so the demo works even on mute
- **Pacing** — every cut earns its place; a demo that drags loses the viewer before the payoff

## Visual Design

### Design Principles

**The product is the star.** Motion graphics explain; they never compete with the device on screen.

**Works on mute.** Social feeds autoplay without sound — captions and graphics carry the message.

**First three seconds decide.** The hook appears immediately; details follow in the body of the cut.

**Typography:** Bold, high-contrast captions that read clearly even on a small phone screen — the motion graphics are designed to be read at a glance, not studied.

## Reflections

Editing a product demo taught me that motion design is pacing as much as decoration. The graphics exist to keep the viewer oriented and the rhythm tight — if a viewer can watch with the sound off and still understand the product, the edit is doing its job.
