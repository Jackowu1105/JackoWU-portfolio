## MTR Property App — Unified Platform with Dynamic UI for 28 Residential Estates

### 📌 Project Overview

MTR Corporation manages 28 residential estates across Hong Kong, each with its own distinct community identity and service needs. Prior to this project, residents had to navigate fragmented digital touchpoints — ranging from physical notice boards to separate estate-specific platforms — leading to inconsistent user experiences and low engagement with community services.

**The Challenge:** How might we create a unified digital platform for all 28 MTR estates that maintains consistent functionality while respecting each estate's unique identity and fostering a sense of community belonging?

**My Role:** I led the UI/UX design for this project, collaborating with 2 developers, 1 project manager, and stakeholders from MTR Corporation and estate management teams. My responsibilities included:
- User research and problem definition
- Information architecture and wireframing
- Visual design and prototyping (Adobe XD)
- Usability testing and iteration
- Design system creation for 28 estate themes

---

## 🔍 Problem & Goals

### User Pain Points

1. **Fragmented access:** Residents managing multiple estate accounts had no unified platform
2. **Lack of community identity:** Generic app interfaces failed to reflect the unique character of each estate
3. **Switching friction:** Multi-estate owners (12% of residents) had to log in and out repeatedly
4. **Low engagement:** Residents relied on outdated methods (physical boards, phone calls) due to inconsistent digital experiences

### Business Problems
- Low adoption rate of digital services across MTR estates
- High operational costs maintaining separate communication channels
- Inconsistent brand experience across residential communities
- Difficulty scaling services to new estates

### Success Metrics
- App adoption rate across 28 estates
- User satisfaction score (post-launch survey)
- Reduction in call center inquiries
- Engagement rate with estate-specific services

## 🔎 Research & Insights

### Research Methods

1. **Stakeholder interviews** with MTR estate managers (28 estates) to understand operational needs
2. **User interviews** with 15 residents from 5 different estates to identify pain points
3. **Competitive analysis** of property management apps (e.g., Link, HKHA) to benchmark features
4. **Contextual inquiry** observing how residents currently access estate information

### Key Findings
- ✅ **Identity matters:** Residents feel strong attachment to their specific estate and want visual differentiation
- ✅ **Consistency is crucial:** While customization is valued, core navigation and features must remain predictable
- ✅ **Multi-estate users exist:** ~12% of residents manage accounts for multiple properties
- ✅ **Mobile-first behavior:** 85% of residents prefer mobile apps over websites for quick tasks

### User Persona: "Mrs. Chan — The Multi-Estate Owner"
- **Age:** 45, owns units in 2 MTR estates
- **Pain point:** "I have to log in and out constantly, and I forget which app is for which estate."
- **Goal:** Quick access to both estates' info without confusion

## 💡 Design Process

### Information Architecture

I defined a flexible IA that:
- Separates **universal features** (maintenance requests, facility booking) from **estate-specific content** (local events, notices)
- Allows content modules to be dynamically loaded based on the user's estate

### Wireframes & Iterations

**Version 1:** Single static home page
→ **User feedback:** "Feels generic, doesn't reflect my estate."

**Version 2:** Manual estate selection at launch
→ **User feedback:** "Extra step is annoying."

**Version 3:** Auto-detected estate with dynamic UI
→ **User feedback:** "Feels like it's made for my community!" ✅

### Key Design Decisions

**1. Dynamic Color Theme System**

**Why:** Each MTR estate has its own brand colors and identity. A generic color scheme would dilute the sense of community.

**How:** I created a theming system with 28 color palettes mapped to estate IDs. The app fetches the appropriate palette on login and applies it to UI components.

**Trade-off:** Required rigorous color contrast testing to ensure WCAG 2.1 AA compliance across all themes.

**2. Seamless Estate Switching**

**Why:** 12% of users manage multiple estates. Logging out and back in creates unnecessary friction.

**How:** I designed a switcher in the profile menu that instantly changes the UI theme and content without re-authentication.

**Validation:** Usability testing showed **90% task completion rate** for multi-estate users.

### Usability Testing
- Conducted **3 rounds** of testing with **25 residents** total
- Tested task flows: login, service request, facility booking, estate switching
- Iterated on visual hierarchy and button placement based on feedback

## 🧠 Lessons Learned

1. **Personalization does not equal complexity:** Dynamic UI can enhance UX without overwhelming users if the system is well-architected.

2. **Design for edge cases early:** Multi-estate users were a minority but had the most critical pain points.

3. **Accessibility testing is non-negotiable:** With 28 color themes, rigorous contrast testing was essential to avoid excluding users.
