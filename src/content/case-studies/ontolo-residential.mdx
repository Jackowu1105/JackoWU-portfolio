## ONTOLO Residential App — Designing an Adaptive Interface for Multi-Generational Users

### Project Overview

Great Eagle Holdings wanted to launch a unified resident app to improve community engagement and streamline property services.

**My Contributions:**
- Led end-to-end UX research and design process
- Conducted user interviews with 20+ residents across diverse age groups
- Designed and validated dual-interface system through iterative usability testing
- Collaborated with developers to implement WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility standards
- Created comprehensive design system with 42+ adaptive components

**Team:** 1 Product Manager, 2 Developers, 1 UX Researcher
**Platform:** iOS & Android

---

## The Challenge

Initial research revealed a critical problem: **one-size-fits-all interfaces failed to serve their diverse resident demographics.**

### User Pain Points

👴 **Elderly residents (60+):**
- Found typical apps overwhelming with too many options
- Struggled with small touch targets (average accuracy: 55%)
- Experienced anxiety about making mistakes
- Often abandoned tasks due to complex navigation

👶 **Children (8-12):**
- Needed supervision-friendly features with limited access to sensitive functions
- Required simpler task flows

👨 **Adult residents:**
- Required full access to all community services and account management
- Expected modern, feature-rich experience

### Business Problems
- Low adoption rates for existing property management apps in the market (&lt;40%)
- High support costs due to usability issues (~$250K annually)
- Risk of excluding elderly residents from digital community engagement
- Competitive disadvantage in luxury property market

### Success Metrics
- Achieve 70%+ adoption rate across all age groups within 6 months
- Reduce support calls related to app usage by 50%
- Maintain 4.5+ star rating in app stores
- Simplified mode usage rate >30% among target demographics

---

## Research & Discovery

### Research Methods

**1. Contextual Inquiries (15 sessions)**
- Observed elderly residents in their homes attempting to use competitor apps
- **Key finding:** 80% abandoned tasks due to accidental taps and navigation confusion
- Discovered users relied heavily on family members for basic app functions

**2. User Interviews (20 participants)**
- Ages 8-75, balanced across demographics and digital literacy levels
- **Revealed:** Elderly users wanted independence but feared making irreversible mistakes
- Children preferred visual, icon-based navigation over text

**3. Competitive Analysis**
- Analyzed 8 property management and senior-friendly apps
- **Gap identified:** No existing solution offered true adaptive complexity
- Apps were either over-simplified (patronizing) or over-complex (overwhelming)

**4. Usability Testing (Existing Solutions)**
- Tested 2 competing apps with target users
- **Result:** Average task success rate only 45% for elderly users
- Task completion time 3x longer than adult users

### Key Insights

💡 **Insight #1: Cognitive Load vs. Capability**
Elderly users aren't "bad with technology" — they're overwhelmed by unnecessary complexity. When we stripped interfaces to core functions in early prototypes, task completion rates jumped from 45% to 92%.

💡 **Insight #2: Control & Dignity**
Users strongly resisted apps marketed as "elderly-friendly" that felt patronizing or limited. The ability to switch between complexity levels empowered users to choose based on comfort, not age.

💡 **Insight #3: Pareto Principle in Features**
Across all user groups, 5 functions accounted for 85% of app usage:
1. Emergency contacts
2. Facility booking
3. Community announcements
4. Visitor access management
5. Service requests

These insights formed our core design principle: **"Simplify by default, expand on demand."**

## Design Process

### Information Architecture

**Solution: Adaptive Interface System**

Rather than creating separate apps (which would stigmatize users and increase costs), I designed a single app with two adaptive modes users can switch between:

**Simplified Mode:**
- 5 core functions prominently displayed
- 60x60pt minimum touch targets (36% larger than iOS standard)
- WCAG AAA color contrast (7:1 ratio)
- Maximum 2-level navigation depth
- High-contrast visual design
- Reduced cognitive load through progressive disclosure

**Full Mode:**
- Complete feature set (15+ functions)
- Standard iOS/Android UI patterns
- Advanced account management
- Multi-step workflows for power users

### Iterations

**Iteration 1: Concept Validation**

*Problem:* Users didn't understand the mode-switching concept during onboarding
*Solution:* Designed adaptive onboarding flow that suggests appropriate mode based on user's stated needs and comfort level
*Result:* Mode comprehension improved from 54% to 89%

**Iteration 2: Information Architecture**

*Problem:* Simplified mode still felt cluttered with 8 visible functions
*Solution:* Reduced to 5 core functions on home screen, moved secondary features to clearly labeled "More" section
*Testing Result:* Task completion time reduced by 38%, user confidence scores increased by 42%

**Iteration 3: Mode Switching Safety**

*Problem:* 15% of users accidentally switched modes mid-task, causing confusion
*Solution:* Added confirmation dialog + moved mode toggle from home screen to settings with clear explanation
*Result:* Accidental switches dropped from 15% to &lt;2%

### Key Design Decisions

**Decision #1: Why Dual Mode vs. Separate Apps?**

After stakeholder workshops and cost-benefit analysis:

✅ **Unified app approach:**
- Prevents stigmatization of elderly users
- Reduces development/maintenance costs by 40%
- Allows families to provide support across modes
- Single codebase for faster iteration

❌ **Separate apps would:**
- Create fragmented user experience
- Double QA and maintenance effort
- Stigmatize users of "senior app"

**Decision #2: Touch Target Sizing**

iOS Standard: 44x44pt → Simplified Mode: 60x60pt minimum

**Rationale:** Motor control research shows 68% accuracy improvement for users with reduced dexterity. Collaborated with developers to implement dynamic spacing algorithms that prevent button overlap.

**Decision #3: Color & Contrast Standards**

- **Simplified mode:** WCAG AAA (7:1 contrast) for all interactive elements
- **Full mode:** WCAG AA (4.5:1 contrast)

**Testing validation:** ColorOracle simulations + testing with 5 colorblind users showed 25% faster element recognition in simplified mode.

**Decision #4: Navigation Depth Reduction**

Reduced navigation hierarchy from 3 levels (Full mode) to maximum 2 levels (Simplified mode).

**Impact:** 53% reduction in navigation errors, 38% faster task completion.

## Visual Design

### Design System

Created a scalable, mode-aware design system:

**Typography Scale:**
- Simplified: 18pt minimum body text (WCAG AAA)
- Full: 14pt minimum body text (WCAG AA)
- Dynamic Type support for user preferences

**Color Palette:**
- Primary: Deep blue (#0A2F5A) — trust & stability
- Accent: Warm orange (#FF7B3D) — approachable & energetic
- High-contrast variants automatically applied in simplified mode
- Passed Deuteranopia, Protanopia, Tritanopia simulations

**Component Library:**
- 42 components with intelligent mode-specific variants
- Auto-scales spacing, padding, and interactive states based on mode
- Consistent 8pt grid system with 16pt unit for simplified mode

**Accessibility Features Implemented:**
- VoiceOver/TalkBack optimization with custom hints
- Dynamic Type support (up to 200% scale)
- Reduced motion options for animation-sensitive users
- Haptic feedback for critical actions

**Visual Highlights:**

1. **Onboarding Flow:** Adaptive mode suggestion based on user's self-reported needs
2. **Home Screen Comparison:** Side-by-side simplified vs. full mode demonstrating information hierarchy
3. **Facility Booking Flow:** Reduced from 5 steps (Full) to 3 steps (Simplified)
4. **Emergency Contacts:** Large, high-contrast emergency button in simplified mode
5. **Settings Panel:** Clear mode-switching interface with explanation and preview

## Lessons Learned

**1. Inclusive Design does not equal Simplified Design**

**Learning:** All users benefit from clarity, but power users still need depth. True accessibility means choice, not reduction.

**Next time:** Consider progressive disclosure systems beyond binary modes — perhaps 3-4 complexity levels, or AI-driven adaptive interfaces that adjust automatically.

**2. Empowerment Through Control**

**Learning:** Giving users control over interface complexity reduced anxiety and increased confidence more than any single design pattern.

**Impact:** This insight influenced my approach to future projects — always provide users agency over their experience.

**3. Data-Driven Iteration Was Critical**

**Learning:** Quantitative usability testing revealed friction points that qualitative interviews missed. The combination of both methods was essential.

**Next time:** Integrate analytics instrumentation earlier in the design process, even in prototypes, to gather behavioral data alongside feedback.

**4. Accessibility Benefits Everyone**

**Learning:** Features designed for elderly users (larger touch targets, clearer labels, reduced complexity) improved usability scores across all age groups by 18%.

**Takeaway:** Accessibility constraints drive better design decisions for everyone.

## Reflection

This project fundamentally shaped my philosophy as a designer: **good design isn't about making things beautiful — it's about making things work for real people with real constraints.**

The ONTOLO app taught me that the most elegant solution isn't always the most technically sophisticated one. Sometimes, it's the one that gives users the power to choose their own path.

**Most importantly:** Designing for elderly users isn't about "dumbing down" interfaces — it's about respecting their intelligence while removing unnecessary friction. When we got this balance right, we didn't just create an accessible app; we created an empowering one.
