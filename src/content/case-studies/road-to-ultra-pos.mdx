## Research & Discovery

I conducted **stakeholder interviews** with event organizers, **vendor interviews** at previous music festivals (5 vendors), **observational research** at similar events, and **competitive analysis** of 3 festival payment systems (Coachella, Tomorrowland, Clockenflap).

### Key Insights

- **Speed is everything:** Attendees tolerate max 2 minutes of waiting. Every second counts.
- **Minimal training required:** Vendors are temporary staff with limited tech experience
- **Harsh environment:** Direct sunlight makes screens hard to read, 85+ dB noise, vendors work 8-hour shifts
- **Two mental models:** Attendees think "How much do I have left?"; Vendors think "Did the payment go through?"

## Design Process

### Information Architecture

Mapped 2 primary user flows:

**Flow 1: Vendor Transaction** — Idle → RFID Scan → Enter Amount → Confirmation → Success/Error

**Flow 2: Balance Check** — RFID Scan → Display Balance

### Wireframing & Iteration

**Round 1 (Paper sketches):** Initial design required 3 taps per transaction — too slow. Removed unnecessary confirmation step.

**Round 2 (Digital prototypes):** Numeric keypad caused input errors in rushed environment. Simplified to preset amount buttons + custom amount option.

**Round 3 (High-fidelity):** Default color scheme barely visible in direct sunlight. Increased contrast to WCAG AAA standards.

### Key Design Decisions

**Single-Tap Transaction Confirmation:** Vendors processed 12 transactions/minute with 1-tap vs. 8 with 2-tap. Risk of accidental taps mitigated by visible "Cancel" button during processing, haptic feedback, and undo function.

**Extra-Large Touch Targets (60x60pt):** Standard 44pt showed 18% error rate during outdoor testing due to wristbands, rushed interactions, and fatigue. Error rate dropped to 3% with 60pt targets.

**Persistent Balance Display:** Balance always visible in top-right corner of vendor screen to address attendee anxiety about running out of tokens.

## Visual Design

**Color Palette:** Road To Ultra brand orange (#FF6B00) for primary action, high-contrast green (#00D084) for success, 21:1 contrast ratio (AAA) for all primary UI. 48pt bold for token amounts optimized for quick scanning in bright outdoor light.

**Components:** 60x60pt minimum touch targets, pulsing RFID scan indicator, multi-modal feedback (visual + haptic), bilingual English/Traditional Chinese support.

## Impact

The system processed **15,247 transactions** over 2 days with **8.2-second average transaction time** (82% faster than cash), **99.3% system uptime**, and **91% vendor satisfaction**. Reconciliation time reduced by 76% (45min to 11min per booth). System was reused for Road To Ultra 2017, 2018, and 2019 events, and expanded to 3 other music festivals in Asia.

## Reflections

Early user research paid off — understanding the harsh festival environment led to crucial design decisions like extra-large touch targets and high-contrast colors. Testing in simulated environments wasn't enough; we had to test in direct sunlight to uncover visibility issues. Finding creative solutions rather than accepting false trade-offs (like speed vs. error prevention) was the key breakthrough.
