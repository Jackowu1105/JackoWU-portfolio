## Research & Discovery

I conducted **stakeholder interviews** with event organisers, **vendor interviews** drawing on previous music-festival experience, **observational research** at similar events, and **competitive analysis** of festival payment systems (Coachella, Tomorrowland, Clockenflap).

### Key Insights

- **Speed is everything** — attendees tolerate only a short wait; every second counts
- **Minimal training required** — vendors are temporary staff with limited tech experience
- **Harsh environment** — direct sunlight makes screens hard to read, noise is high, and vendors work long shifts
- **Two mental models** — attendees think "How much do I have left?"; vendors think "Did the payment go through?"

## Design Process

### Information Architecture

Mapped 2 primary user flows:

**Flow 1: Vendor transaction** — Idle → RFID Scan → Enter Amount → Confirmation → Success/Error

**Flow 2: Balance check** — RFID Scan → Display Balance

### Key Design Decisions

**Single-tap transaction confirmation** — One-tap confirmation sped up transactions meaningfully versus a two-tap flow. The risk of accidental taps was mitigated by a visible "Cancel" button during processing, haptic feedback, and an undo function.

**Extra-large touch targets** — Standard-size targets showed a high error rate during outdoor testing (wristbands, rushed interactions, fatigue). Enlarging the targets cut the error rate sharply.

**Persistent balance display** — Balance is always visible in the top-right corner of the vendor screen to address attendee anxiety about running out of tokens.

## Visual Design

### Color

A deep, dark base (`#1B112C` / `#130E1F`) — a festival-night purple that doubles as a high-contrast surface for outdoor screens — with a high-visibility green for success states and AAA-level contrast on primary UI. The dark backdrop lets the brand accent and token amounts read clearly even in direct sunlight.

### Typography & Components

Bold typography for token amounts, optimised for quick scanning in bright outdoor light. Extra-large touch targets, a pulsing RFID scan indicator, multi-modal feedback (visual + haptic), and bilingual English/Traditional Chinese support.

## Outcomes & Feedback

The cashless system kept queues moving through peak hours, vendors picked it up with minimal training, and reconciliation at close became far faster than counting cash. The system was reused for later Road To Ultra events and expanded to other music festivals.

## Reflections

Early user research paid off — understanding the harsh festival environment led to crucial design decisions like extra-large touch targets and high-contrast colors. Testing in simulated environments wasn't enough; we had to test in direct sunlight to uncover visibility issues. Finding creative solutions rather than accepting false trade-offs (like speed vs. error prevention) was the key breakthrough.
