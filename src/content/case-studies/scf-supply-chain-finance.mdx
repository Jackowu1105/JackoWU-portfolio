## Problem Definition

Supply chain finance platforms are data-heavy environments where users typically navigate through deep menu hierarchies to find the asset or calculation they need. Two failure modes follow:

- **The menu maze** — users hunt through nested navigation to find a specific asset or run a calculation
- **The spreadsheet trap** — the data is all there, but only a finance professional can read it efficiently

### Design Goals

- **LLM-first home** — users ask about assets in natural language, the app answers and acts
- **Structured companions** — an asset list and asset calculator sit alongside the chat for users who prefer to browse
- **Dark/light theming** — a glass-morphism sidebar and consistent token system across both modes

## Design Process

### Information Architecture

The app is structured around three primary surfaces:

1. **LLM Assistant (Home)** — the front door; users ask about assets in natural language and the app responds with data, charts, and actions
2. **Asset List** — a structured, searchable list of all assets for users who prefer to browse rather than ask
3. **Asset Calculator** — a tool for running calculations on assets, accessible from both the assistant (as a referenced action) and independently

The decision to put the LLM assistant at the front door — rather than a traditional dashboard — reflects the product's thesis: the assistant is the primary interface, the structured tools are the reference.

## Visual Design

### Color

A clean, light system with a glass-morphism sidebar — light blue and white surfaces (`#FDFEFF` / `#E4F0F6`) with a confident cyan primary (`#44BBDC` / `#A6D0E0`) for actions and active states. The glass effect on the sidebar adds depth without competing with the data.

### Typography

A system sans-serif with a clear hierarchy — conversational text for the LLM responses, structured data for the asset list, and clear numeric formatting for the calculator.

## Reflections

Designing an LLM-first finance tool taught me that the conversational interface and the structured tools need to coexist, not compete. The assistant is the front door, but the asset list and calculator are there for users who want to browse — and the assistant can reference them directly, so the two paths stay connected.
