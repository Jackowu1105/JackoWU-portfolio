## Problem Definition

A datathon sits between a hackathon and an official government-backed event — the identity needed to feel credible (government co-organiser) and energetic (a competition) at the same time. The same system had to work on a backdrop people see from across a hall and on a certificate they keep in a folder.

### Design Goals

- Build a cohesive visual identity across backdrop, roll-up, rundown, and certificate
- Balance an official tone with the energy of a competition
- Make the system flexible enough that every material reads as one event

## Design Process

### Visual Direction

I explored directions around movement, data flow, and logistics networks, and refined toward a system that pairs a confident blue with a vivid accent — official enough for the government co-organiser, energetic enough for a competition.

### Materials

I designed and produced the key event materials, each testing the identity at a different scale:

- **Backdrop** — the event's main visual anchor, tested at large-format scale
- **Roll-up** — portable signage, testing legibility at distance
- **Rundown** — the schedule, testing information hierarchy and readability
- **Certificate** — the takeaway, testing the brand at a small, keepsake scale

## Visual Design

### Color

A confident blue primary (`#2E64A7` / `#3170C1`) carries the official, institutional tone, paired with a vivid pink-red accent (`#D93E61`) for the competition energy, all anchored by a deep navy (`#2D3046`) for type and structure. The blue/pink pairing reads as modern and data-forward without feeling corporate.

### Typography

A clean sans-serif with a strong hierarchy — large display weights for the event title, a highly legible sans for schedules and body — chosen to reproduce cleanly across both print and digital materials.

## Reflections

An event identity is really a test of scalability — the same system has to work on a backdrop people see from across a hall and on a certificate they keep in a folder. Building the hierarchy and color rules up front meant every material was a configuration of the system, not a fresh design problem.
