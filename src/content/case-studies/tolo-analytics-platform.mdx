## Problem Definition

A trade finance analytics platform's website has two jobs: convince a visitor the product is credible, and show them what it actually does. The challenge was that trade finance terms are opaque to the business owners who need them most — a credit calculator was needed to make financing cost, limit, and term comparable at a glance.

### Design Goals

- **Communicate credibility** — trust signals (partner logos, university affiliations) structured into the home and About Us
- **Make the product legible** — a credit calculator that turns opaque financing terms into something a business owner can evaluate
- **Connect to the product line** — TOLOAnalytics is the marketing site to TOLOD's operations app; the two should feel like one product family

## Design Process

### Information Architecture

The site is structured around three primary surfaces:

1. **Home** — the front door, structured around trust signals (partner logos, university affiliations) and a clear value proposition
2. **About Us** — the credibility layer, with team, partners, and institutional backing
3. **Credit Calculator** — the interactive tool that makes financing cost, limit, and term comparable at a glance

## Visual Design

### Color

A clean, institutional system — white and soft blue surfaces (`#FDFDFD` / `#8CAFC3`) with a confident blue primary (`#5183A2` / `#33577B`) that carries the professional, trustworthy tone. The palette is calm and credible without feeling corporate-stale.

### Typography

A system sans-serif with a clear hierarchy — large display weights for the value proposition, a highly legible sans for body and the calculator interface.

## Reflections

Designing a trade finance analytics website taught me that credibility is the product. The credit calculator did more than any feature list could — it turned the product's abstract value into something a business owner could touch and evaluate. And structuring the home around trust signals before features addressed the real barrier: not "what does it do?" but "can I trust it?"
