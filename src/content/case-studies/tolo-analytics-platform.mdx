## Problem Definition

A trade-finance lending decision is a chain of trust checks. The system's job is to make that trust cheap to verify — so the risk officer's attention goes to the decision, not to finding the documents.

### Design Goals

- **Make the dashboard a to-do list, not a chart wall** — pending applications, disbursements, repayments, and risk alerts surface first; charts support, they don't lead
- **Keep due diligence deep but navigable** — KYC / KYS / KYP as layered drill-downs from one customer record, not separate silos
- **Compress risk into a scorecard that can always show its working** — the evidence a click below the number
- **Put the portfolio decision in context** — assemble loan portfolios against the same customer and risk data the underwriters see

## Design Process

### Information Architecture

The system is structured around the lending journey — each surface maps to one step in it:

1. **Dashboard (整體看板)** — the front door. Pending work leads: loan applications awaiting review, disbursements and repayments to process, customers awaiting credit-limit decisions, overdue loans, and customers triggering risk alerts. KPI rows keep loan volumes, balances, customer counts, and settlement health in view; a 7-day trend chart shows new loans; an active-loan donut splits the book by status (overdue / disbursed / pending disbursement / pending review); a recent-KYC-updates list keeps underwriting context fresh.
2. **Customer List (客戶列表)** — the portfolio in one scrollable, filterable surface. Every borrower reachable, every detail a click away.
3. **KYC / KYS / KYP detail layers** — from a customer record, the underwriter drills down: corporate identity (KYC), store operations (KYS), and product provenance (KYP). Three levels of verification, one thread of decision.
4. **Scorecard** — the risk decision, compressed into a number, with the evidence layers beneath it.
5. **Portfolio (投資組合)** — selection and assembly of loan portfolios, sitting on the same data the underwriting screens use.

### The embedded assistant

The dashboard carries an **Ask TOLO AI** panel — a plain-language assistant inside the workspace, with suggested questions to start from. The bet: a risk officer should be able to ask about the book in the same language they think in, without leaving the dashboard.

## Visual Design

### Color

The system is a single-brand blue on white. The brand blue (`#1078C0`) carries actions and emphasis; pale blue surfaces (`#C2DCEE`) keep the workspace calm; neutral ink (`#313233`) keeps tables and data legible. In a lending workspace, colour is status and signal — overdue reads warm, everything else stays cool and credible.

### Typography

A clean system sans with a strong numeric treatment — loan amounts, counts, and scorecard numbers get display weight, while table data stays tight and highly legible. The hierarchy is engineered for scanning: KPIs read in a glance, documents in focus.

## Public Website

Alongside the platform, I designed the public marketing website — the story told to prospective borrowers. Two pages (Home + About Us), each with a full mobile version, built as a scroll that leads from trust to action.

### A deeper register

The website deliberately steps away from the platform's vivid interactive blue. Where the app needs energy and signal, the marketing site reads as a financial brand — a deep navy carries the hero and sections, with muted steel and sky blues for accents.

## Reflections

Designing TOLO Analytics taught me that in trade finance, the interface is a trust machine. The dashboard's first job is not to look smart — it is to make sure nothing that needs a decision gets missed, which is why the to-do surface leads and the charts follow. The KYC / KYS / KYP layering is the quiet core of the system: it lets an underwriter verify a borrower three levels deep without ever losing the thread of the decision. And the scorecard is only trustworthy because the evidence is always one click away.
