## Problem Definition

A young technology company needs a brand that holds together across every touchpoint — from a 9pt favicon to a 3-metre event backdrop. Without a system, each application drifts and the brand feels improvised.

### Design Goals

- Redesign the logo to be distinctive, scalable, and reflective of the smart-sensing positioning
- Build a style guide that makes every future asset a configuration, not a new decision
- Apply the brand consistently across digital and print — name cards, website, and event materials

## Design Process

### Logo Redesign

I explored directions centred on the idea of "sensing" — marks that suggest detection, signal, or awareness — and refined toward a form that reads clearly at both favicon and backdrop scale. The final mark is built on a clear geometric construction so it can be reproduced accurately across vendors and materials.

### Style Guide

The style guide covers:

- **Logo construction** — grid, proportions, and minimum clear space
- **Color** — primary and secondary palettes with usage rules
- **Typography** — type pairings for headings and body, in print and on screen
- **Application rules** — dos and don'ts for placement, color, and scale

### Cross-Medium Application

I applied the system across four touchpoints to validate it held up:

- **Name cards** — the brand at its smallest, testing legibility and clear space
- **Leaflet** — print layout testing color and type at scale
- **Website** — the brand in motion, testing responsive behavior and digital color
- **Event booth backdrop** — the brand at its largest, testing impact and readability from distance

## Visual Design

### Color

A restrained, modern neutral palette — soft greys (`#D7D5D7` / `#C1BDC3`) as the surface, a near-black (`#171116`) for type and the logo's anchor, and a muted mid-grey (`#A9A6AA`) for secondary elements. The restraint keeps the focus on the logo mark and lets it carry the brand across every medium.

### Typography

A clean sans-serif pairing — a geometric sans for the logo and headings, a highly legible sans for body text — chosen to reproduce cleanly in both print and on screen.

## Outcomes & Feedback

The redesigned logo and style guide gave ubiZense a consistent visual identity across every touchpoint — name cards, website, and event materials all read as one brand, and the guide made new assets fast to produce without reinventing the system each time.

## Reflections

A brand system is only as good as its application rules. The biggest test wasn't the logo itself but whether it held up from a 9pt favicon to a 3-metre backdrop — building that scalability in from the construction grid saved every downstream application from becoming a special case.
