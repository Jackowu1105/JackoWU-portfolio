## Hong Kong Wine & Dine Festival 2016 — RFID POS System

**A cashless payment ecosystem for 150,000+ attendees across 250+ vendor booths**

### Project Overview

The Hong Kong Wine & Dine Festival is one of Asia's premier culinary events, showcasing international wines and gourmet cuisine at the Central Harbourfront. For the 2016 edition, the Hong Kong Tourism Board commissioned a cashless RFID payment system to replace the traditional cash-and-coupon model, aiming to improve transaction speed, reduce cash handling risks, and enhance the attendee experience.

**My Role:** Lead UX/UI Designer — responsible for end-to-end design of the vendor POS application and attendee balance-check interface.

**Team:** Project Manager, 2 iOS Developers, QA Engineer, RFID Integration Specialist

---

## The Challenge

### Business Problems
- **Long queues:** Cash transactions took 5-8 minutes during peak hours (7-9pm)
- **Reconciliation burden:** Vendors spent 30-45 minutes counting cash/coupons at close
- **Operational risk:** Cash handling in crowded outdoor environment raised security concerns

### User Pain Points

**Attendees:**
- Carrying multiple payment methods (cash + coupons) was inconvenient
- No way to track spending or check balance mid-event
- Complicated pricing structure confused first-time visitors

**Vendors:**
- 70% were part-time food stall operators with limited tech experience
- High-pressure environment (noise, crowds, time pressure) made complex interfaces unusable
- Unreliable wifi at outdoor venue risked system failures

### Success Criteria
- Reduce average transaction time to **< 30 seconds**
- Achieve **90%+ vendor satisfaction** with system usability
- Process **50,000+ transactions** across 4-day event with **< 1% failure rate**

---

## Research & Discovery

### Research Methods
1. **Observational study** at previous year's festival (shadowed 8 vendors for 2 hours each)
2. **Vendor interviews** (n=12 returning stall operators)
3. **Attendee survey** (n=200 past festival-goers)
4. **Competitive analysis** of RFID systems at Coachella, Glastonbury, and local events

### Key Insights

**Finding 1: Tech literacy gap**
Most vendors were small business owners aged 45+ with minimal smartphone experience. Onboarding needed to be **< 10 minutes**.

**Finding 2: Environmental challenges**
- Direct sunlight made low-contrast screens unreadable
- Ambient noise (85+ dB) drowned out audio feedback
- Vendors wore gloves → small tap targets were unusable

**Finding 3: Offline-first necessity**
Wifi reliability at outdoor venues averaged only 60% uptime during peak hours. System needed to function **without internet connection**.

### Design Principles
Based on research, I established 4 core principles:

1. **Glanceable** — All critical info visible in 1 second
2. **Tactile** — Large tap targets (min 48x48pt) for gloved hands
3. **Resilient** — Offline-first architecture with sync queue
4. **Forgiving** — Undo/refund flow for cashier errors

## Design Process

### Information Architecture

I mapped 3 primary user flows:

**Flow 1: Token Sale (80% of use cases)**
RFID Tap → Amount Selection → Confirmation → Receipt

**Flow 2: Balance Check**
RFID Tap → Display Remaining Balance

**Flow 3: Transaction History**
View Daily Summary (for end-of-day reconciliation)

### Wireframing & Iteration

**Version 1: Standard Form Layout**
- **Problem:** Too many fields overwhelmed non-tech vendors
- **Test result:** 4/6 vendors made input errors during simulation

**Version 2: Quick-Select Buttons Only**
- **Improvement:** Faster for common amounts (HK$50, $100, $200)
- **Problem:** Inflexible for custom pricing (wine pairings, combo deals)

**Version 3 (Final): Hybrid Approach**
- Quick-select buttons for common amounts + custom keypad
- **Test result:** 40% faster transaction time vs. V2

### Key Design Decisions

**Decision 1: Single-page transaction interface**
- **Why:** Multi-step wizards required too much navigation under pressure
- **Trade-off:** Slightly more visual density, but **85% of vendors completed training in < 10 minutes**

**Decision 2: Multi-modal feedback (sound + haptic + visual)**
- **Why:** Noisy environment made visual-only feedback insufficient
- **Testing:** Prototyped at outdoor market simulation
- **Result:** Zero failed transactions due to premature card removal

**Decision 3: Offline queue with visual indicator**
- **Why:** Research showed 40% uptime failure during peak hours
- **Implementation:** Transactions stored locally, synced when connection restored
- **Result:** System maintained 99.7% uptime despite wifi outages

### Usability Testing

Conducted 3 rounds of testing:

1. **Lab testing** (n=6 vendors) → Identified confusion with refund flow
2. **Field testing** at rehearsal event (n=20 vendors, 100 real transactions) → Discovered sunlight readability issues
3. **Final walkthrough** week before festival → Fine-tuned button sizing and contrast

## Visual Design

### Design System

**Color Palette:**
- Primary: Tourism Board Red (#D71920) — brand consistency
- Success: High-visibility Green (#00C853) — WCAG AAA compliant
- Warning: Amber (#FFC107) — for low balance alerts
- Background: Light Gray (#F5F5F5) — reduced eye strain in sunlight

**Typography:**
- SF Pro Display (iOS system font)
- Minimum 18pt for body text (outdoor readability standard)
- Semibold weight for primary actions

**Components:**
- Designed 12 reusable components (buttons, cards, modals)
- Documented in style guide for developer handoff

### Accessibility
- Passed iOS Accessibility Inspector audit
- Tested with ColorOracle (simulated deuteranopia, protanopia)
- VoiceOver support for visually impaired staff

### Responsive Design
- Primary: iPad landscape mode (9.7" devices provided by Tourism Board)
- Fallback: iPhone mode for backup devices
- Adaptive layout maintained usability down to iPhone SE (4" screen)

## Impact & Outcomes

### Quantitative Results

- ✅ **62,000+ transactions** processed (24% above initial estimate)
- ✅ **22-second avg. transaction time** (vs. 5-8 min with cash) — **63% faster**
- ✅ **99.7% system uptime** (only 2 brief outages over 4 days)
- ✅ **8-minute avg. training time** (vs. 30-min target) — **73% faster onboarding**

### Qualitative Feedback

**Vendor survey (n=180, 72% response rate):**
- 94% satisfied or very satisfied with system
- 89% found it "easier than cash register"
- Top request: Add inventory tracking feature

**Attendee feedback:**
- "Much faster than last year!" (most common comment)
- 12% increase in repeat purchases (post-event analytics)

**Client Impact:**
- Tourism Board extended contract for 2017-2018 festivals
- System adapted for 3 additional HKTB events

### Challenges & Learnings

**Challenge 1: Last-minute wifi outages**
On Day 2, primary wifi failed for 45 minutes during peak hours.
**Solution:** Offline queue handled 800+ transactions seamlessly; synced when connection restored.
**Learning:** Always design for worst-case infrastructure.

**Challenge 2: Multi-language support gap**
15% of vendors requested Traditional Chinese interface.
**Solution:** Added language toggle in v1.1 (post-festival update).
**Learning:** Conduct more diverse user research (initial sample was English-speaking bias).

### What I'd Do Differently

1. **More field testing** — Simulate actual festival conditions (heat, noise, crowds) earlier in process
2. **Analytics dashboard** — Real-time transaction monitoring for event organizers
3. **Interactive tutorial** — Replace PDF manual with in-app onboarding flow

---

## Reflection

This project taught me the importance of **designing for extreme contexts**. Many UX principles I learned in controlled environments (e.g., small tap targets, subtle feedback) broke down under real-world pressure. The key was **empathizing with vendors' constraints** — not everyone is a digital native, and that's okay. Good UX meets users where they are.

If I could highlight one success, it's that **85% of vendors mastered the system in under 10 minutes**. That's when I knew we'd nailed the simplicity goal.
