## zProduct — Redesigning a Universal Airport Logistics Dashboard

### Overview

zProduct is a universal airport logistics management system serving airports of all sizes worldwide. The existing AS2-based dashboard offered a rigid, one-size-fits-all interface that failed to meet the diverse operational needs of different airports. My goal was to redesign the dashboard into a flexible, configurable system that empowers users to customize their workspace while maintaining high performance and usability.

**Team:** 1 UX Designer (me), 2 Frontend Developers, 1 Product Manager

---

## The Problem

### User Pain Points

Through interviews with 12 airport operations managers and analysis of 6 months of AS2 usage data, I identified three critical problems:

**1. Limited Configurability (Impact: High)**
The AS2 dashboard locked users into a fixed layout, preventing them from prioritizing information relevant to their specific airport operations. 68% of users reported difficulty finding critical information quickly, leading to delayed decision-making during peak hours.

**2. Data Fragmentation (Impact: High)**
Users had to toggle between 3-5 separate systems to gather data on cargo, gates, OTP (On-Time Performance), and bay status. This fragmentation increased average decision-making time by 40% and created opportunities for errors.

**3. Resource Management Challenges (Impact: Medium)**
Without integrated tools for personnel and logistics coordination, staff relied on manual processes that led to resource allocation errors, resulting in a 23% increase in operational delays.

### Business Impact
- Declining customer satisfaction due to operational inefficiencies
- High training costs for the rigid AS2 system
- Difficulty onboarding new airports to the platform

### Success Metrics
- Reduce task completion time by 30%
- Increase user satisfaction (SUS score) from 52 to 75+
- Decrease training time for new users by 50%

## Research & Discovery

### Research Methods

**1. Contextual Inquiry**
I observed airport staff during peak operational hours, revealing critical workflow bottlenecks. For example, users kept sticky notes of cargo IDs because the system didn't surface this information prominently.

**2. User Interviews (n=12)**
I conducted semi-structured interviews with operations managers, cargo coordinators, and gate supervisors to understand their daily workflows and pain points.

**3. Data Analysis**
By analyzing AS2 usage logs, I identified the most-used features (cargo tracking, gate info) and workflows with high abandonment rates (custom report generation).

**4. Competitive Analysis**
I evaluated competing dashboard solutions to benchmark configurability, data visualization approaches, and performance.

### Key Insights

**1. Different airports have vastly different priorities.**
Small regional airports focus on cargo tracking, while large international hubs prioritize OTP and bay optimization.
→ **Design Implication:** A widget-based system where users can add, remove, and prioritize modules.

**2. Users need at-a-glance status awareness.**
85% of users check the dashboard every 10-15 minutes to monitor operations.
→ **Design Implication:** High-visibility design with color-coded status indicators.

**3. The current system requires too many clicks.**
Users averaged 7 clicks to access cargo tracking data.
→ **Design Implication:** Flatten navigation with customizable layouts.

### User Personas

**Operations Manager Oliver (42)**
Monitors overall airport performance and quickly identifies issues. Needs a high-level, customizable dashboard with KPIs at a glance.

**Cargo Coordinator Clara (35)**
Manages cargo logistics and ground staff coordination. Needs detailed cargo tracking and real-time updates.

## Design Process

### Information Architecture

I restructured the dashboard around a modular widget system:
- **Core Widgets:** Cargo Tracking, Gate Info, OTP, Pass Rate, Bay Insight, Custom Alerts
- **Flexible Hierarchy:** Users can resize and reposition widgets based on priority
- **Reduced Navigation Depth:** From 3-level menu to 1-click widget access

### Wireframes & Iterations

**Low-Fidelity Exploration (Weeks 1-2)**
I sketched 5 different layout concepts and tested paper prototypes with 3 airport managers. Early feedback revealed that a rigid grid felt too restrictive, so I pivoted to a drag-and-drop flexible grid.

**Mid-Fidelity Prototyping (Weeks 3-4)**
I created interactive Figma prototypes to test the widget customization flow with 8 users. A key insight emerged: users wanted preset templates for common airport types (cargo-focused, passenger-focused, mixed operations) to avoid starting from scratch.

**High-Fidelity Refinement (Weeks 5-6)**
I finalized the visual design, built a scalable component library, and refined micro-interactions like drag-and-drop feedback and alert animations.

### Key Design Decisions

**1. Drag-and-Drop Widget System**
- **Why:** Research showed no single layout works for all airports.
- **How:** I implemented a 12-column grid system with flexible widget sizing (small, medium, large).
- **Tradeoff:** Added complexity for first-time setup → Mitigated with an onboarding tour and 5 preset templates.

**2. Color-Coded Status Indicators**
- **Why:** Users need instant status awareness when glancing at the dashboard.
- **How:** I used red (critical), yellow (warning), green (normal) with WCAG AA-compliant contrast ratios.
- **Validation:** Usability testing showed 90% faster anomaly detection compared to AS2.

**3. Separated View and Edit Modes**
- **Why:** Users feared accidentally moving widgets during daily operations.
- **How:** I designed a clear toggle between "View Mode" (locked layout) and "Edit Mode" (customizable).
- **Result:** Zero accidental edits in usability testing.

### Usability Testing

**Round 1: Mid-Fidelity (n=8)**
- Task: Customize dashboard for a cargo-focused airport
- Success Rate: 75%
- Key Issue: Users struggled to find the "Add Widget" button
- Iteration: Made the button more prominent and added "+" icons in empty grid spaces

**Round 2: High-Fidelity (n=10)**
- Task: Rearrange widgets and set up custom alerts
- Success Rate: 95%
- Avg. Completion Time: 3 minutes (down from 7 minutes)
- SUS Score: 78 (vs. 52 in AS2)

## Visual Design

### Design System

**Color Palette**
- Primary Blue (#0066CC): Trust and reliability (aviation industry standard)
- Status Colors: Red (#D32F2F), Yellow (#FFA000), Green (#388E3C)
- Rationale: High contrast for control room environments; tested for color-blind accessibility with Stark.

**Iconography**
I designed a custom icon set on a 24px grid with consistent 2px strokes, optimized for small sizes in widget headers.

**Data/Numbers:** Monospace font for easier scanning of numerical data.

### Accessibility
- **WCAG AA Compliance:** All text meets 4.5:1 contrast ratio
- **Keyboard Navigation:** Full keyboard support for drag-and-drop
- **Screen Reader Support:** Semantic HTML, ARIA labels for dynamic widgets
- **Responsive Design:** Optimized for large control room displays (1920x1080+)

## Lessons Learned

**1. Balance Flexibility with Simplicity**
While power users loved deep customization, some users felt overwhelmed.
**Next Step:** Implement progressive disclosure (basic/advanced modes).

**2. Preset Templates Are Crucial**
Users wanted starting points, not just a blank canvas.
**Next Step:** Expand template library based on airport profiles.

**3. Performance at Scale**
Airports with 20+ widgets experienced slight lag.
**Next Step:** Optimize rendering with lazy loading.
